How the tool works

Infant discrimination data give perceptual dissimilarities between segments, which multidimensional scaling embeds in two or three dimensions. The distance between a pair becomes a Gaussian prior on the weight of the *MAP constraint that maps one segment onto the other.

Use

Choose a class and two segments, then set the hyperparameters. The tool reports the distance d(x, y) and the prior:

μxy = α + γ · d(x, y)    w*Map(x, y) ∼ Normal(μxy, τ2)

Save contrast keeps the current row in your browser and Download CSV exports the saved rows. The address bar mirrors the selection, so the link reproduces it.

Hyperparameters

α and γ define the line: α is the weight at distance 0, usually 0 or a small floor, and γ the weight gained per unit of distance (scaled, the gap between the closest and the farthest pair of the class). τ sets how firmly the prior holds; a small τ resists the data.

What the numbers are

Distance is Euclidean in the chosen embedding. Scaled, it runs from 0 to 1 within the class, so γ means the same for consonants and vowels. Combined |g| is the estimate of infant discriminability on the pooled Hedges' |g| scale, an equal-weight average of three imputation routes. Each route is also shown. For tested pairs the pooled value from the experiments is listed and is what the export carries.

The space

Effect sizes from experiments ( reports, contrasts) were pooled per contrast by random-effects meta-analysis. The universe is the consonants and vowels found in at least 5% of PHOIBLE inventories. The embedding is precision-weighted interval MDS of the metric route's calibrated dissimilarities, which form a proper weighted Euclidean metric; the other two routes enter only the combined estimate. Weighted stress-1: consonants in 2-D and in 3-D, vowels and . Consonants and vowels are embedded separately, so a distance across the two classes is undefined.

Routes

Metric learning. Each segment is a PanPhon feature vector (tone and length removed). A weighted feature distance with a vowel-pair offset is fitted to the pooled |g| of the tested contrasts by precision-weighted least squares. The weights are anchored at PanPhon's defaults with a ridge penalty on their log-multipliers; the ridge strength was chosen among six candidate learners by precision-weighted leave-one-out error with a one-standard-error rule. Uncertainty comes from 200 bootstrap refits.

Natural classes. Natural classes are generated by intersection closure over single feature-value descriptions (15,522 classes over 108 segments). Following Frisch, Pierrehumbert & Broe (2004), similarity is the share of classes two segments have in common; dissimilarity is one minus that share. Nothing here is fitted to infant data.

Bayesian inversion. A hierarchical model estimates over 1,811 languages how a contrast's co-occurrence depends on infant discriminability once genealogical and areal structure are removed. Inverting that regression draw by draw turns a pair's adjusted co-occurrence into a posterior over its expected |g|. The adjustment predicts co-occurrence from joint and independence-expected prevalence, so rare segments are not mistaken for distant ones.

Combination. Each route is affinely calibrated to the pooled infant scale on held-out predictions with a shared vowel offset. A one-standard-error rule preferred the equal-weight average of the three over the best single route.
