What to calculate, how it is calculated, and how to interpret the output

Infant discrimination data give pairwise perceptual dissimilarities between segments. These are embedded in a 2-D or 3-D space by multidimensional scaling, and the distance between a chosen pair is turned into a Gaussian prior on the weight of the corresponding *MAP constraint.

Quick use

Pick a segment class and two segments (Section 01), choose the 2-D or 3-D embedding, then set the prior hyperparameters (Section 02). The tool reports the distance between the two segments in the infant-calibrated perceptual space and turns it into a prior on the corresponding faithfulness constraint:

μxy = α + γ · d(x,y)   and   w*MAP(x,y) ~ Normal(μxy, τ²)

Save contrast stores the current configuration in the table at the bottom (kept in your browser between visits); Download CSV exports everything you saved. The address bar follows your selection, so copying the link shares the exact contrast and settings.

Main outputs

Predicted |g| is the fused estimate of infant discriminability for the selected pair on the pooled Hedges' |g| scale. MDS distance is the Euclidean distance between the two segments in the selected embedding; with min–max scaling on, it is rescaled to [0, 1] over all pairs of the same class so that γ has a class-independent interpretation. Prior mean μ is the expected *MAP weight under your (α, γ).

Practical guidance for α, γ, and τ

α sets the weight expected for a mapping between two identical-sounding segments (distance 0) — usually 0 or a small positive floor. γ sets how steeply expected faithfulness grows with perceptual distance; with min–max scaling it equals the weight difference between the closest and most distant pair. τ expresses confidence: small τ makes the prior firm, large τ lets the data override it easily.

Computation of μ

Distances come from precision-weighted interval multidimensional scaling of the fused dissimilarity matrix (weighted stress-1: consonants 0.18 in 2-D / 0.12 in 3-D; vowels 0.16 / 0.09). Optional min–max scaling maps the class's distance range onto [0, 1]; μ is then the affine map above. Distances between a consonant and a vowel are undefined: the two classes are embedded separately.

Calculation of the MDS spaces

Effect sizes from infant discrimination experiments (228 effects, 88 studies, 64 contrasts) were pooled per contrast by random-effects meta-analysis. Estimates for untested pairs come from three independent imputation routes; each route was calibrated against the pooled infant estimates on held-out contrasts and the routes were combined with cross-validated precision weights. The resulting dissimilarities for all same-class pairs (68 consonants, 18 vowels) were embedded with precision-weighted interval MDS in 2-D and 3-D.

Details about the imputation routes

Route 1 — Metric learning. Each segment is represented by its PanPhon articulatory feature vector (tone and length features removed), and a weighted feature distance dw(a,b) = (Σk wk(xak−xbk)²)1/2 plus a vowel-pair offset is fit to the pooled infant |g| of the tested contrasts by precision-weighted least squares. The per-feature weights are anchored at PanPhon's default salience weights with a ridge penalty on the log-multipliers — free weights overfit at 64 contrasts — and the ridge strength, along with the choice of learner (the anchored metric against ridge regression, k-nearest-neighbours, and tree ensembles), is selected by precision-weighted leave-one-out error with a one-standard-error rule that favours the simplest model. The learned metric then scores every untested pair, with uncertainty from 200 bootstrap refits.

Route 2 — Natural classes. The set of phonological natural classes is generated by intersection closure over all single feature-value descriptions of the full segment inventory (15,522 classes over the 108 segments involved). Following Frisch, Pierrehumbert & Broe (2004), similarity is the number of natural classes containing both segments divided by the number containing either, and the route's dissimilarity is one minus this ratio. This route needs no fitting at all, so it cannot inherit noise from the infant data.

Route 3 — Bayesian inversion. A hierarchical Bayesian model first estimates, over 1,811 languages, how a contrast's cross-linguistic co-occurrence depends on infant discriminability after removing genealogical structure (a 50-tree phylogenetic factor model) and areal structure (a spatial Gaussian-process factor model). That regression is then inverted draw by posterior draw: a pair's adjusted co-occurrence — predicted from its joint and independence-expected prevalence so that segment rarity is not mistaken for dissimilarity — yields a posterior distribution over its expected |g|.

Calibration and fusion. Each route is affinely calibrated to the pooled infant |g| scale on its held-out predictions (with a shared vowel offset), and the three calibrated routes are combined with convex weights chosen by cross-validated precision-weighted error, again with a one-standard-error preference for the simplest combination. Tested contrasts always carry their empirical pooled values in the export.
